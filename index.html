Research Focus

The trajectory from my Master's work to my current PhD research.

My M.Sc. research focused on deep-learning-based classification of Wireless Capsule Endoscopy images — EfficientNet architectures, data augmentation, ensembling, and hyperparameter tuning, reaching 95.75% accuracy. A key outcome of that work: aggregate accuracy alone doesn't fully describe model behavior — a model can score well overall while performing very unevenly across individual classes.

Central PhD research question: how can deep learning models, particularly CNN-based architectures, be structured and designed to achieve more consistent performance across classes while maintaining trustworthy and reliable predictions, especially under limited-data conditions?

This has two main dimensions: class-wise performance consistency (reducing disparities in per-class accuracy rather than relying on aggregate metrics), and trustworthy predictive performance (confidence, uncertainty, calibration, robustness, and knowing when a model may be unreliable). I'm examining how architectural components and learned representations — attention mechanisms and feature representations in particular — can be structured to support both.

Information-theoretic methods aren't the starting point of this problem; they're one potential methodological direction for addressing it. The entropy-regularized channel-attention work is an initial investigation of that direction — the broader question stays independent of any single method, open to other architectural, uncertainty-aware, or representation-learning approaches as the research develops.
